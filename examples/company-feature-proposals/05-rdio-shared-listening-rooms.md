# C05 — Rdio Shared Listening Rooms

Synthetic counterfactual feature proposal for an imagined operating-era Rdio team; not a recovered company document or a present-day launch request. Only the source-linked product context is factual. All feature-specific observations, costs, forecasts, staffing, and constraints below are invented test data.

## Company context

Rdio supplied streaming-music technology. This concept builds on subscription listening and shared music discovery; it does not assume existing catalog contracts permit every proposed use. [Pandora's description of Rdio technology and assets](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1230276/000123027615000046/p-ex991_20151116.htm), November 16, 2015. This is retrospective context for the fictional scenario, not a strict historical evidence cutoff.

## Decision requested

Approve an eight-week, $90,000 launch of shared rooms for 500 households in one territory, which has not yet been selected. Allocate $60,000 engineering, $15,000 licensing review, and $15,000 support and launch. The subscription-product lead owns delivery. Any additional licensing payments are outside the estimate and currently unfunded.

## Customer problem and feature

Friends and family want to discover albums together without coordinating playback by text message. A host creates a room, queues an album, and invites up to five people. Participants can react, discuss songs, and suggest the next track.

The proposed commercial promise is one $15 household subscription covering six simultaneous listeners. Each device plays its own stream; the feature does not rebroadcast an audio feed. The proposal nevertheless assumes that changing device architecture eliminates the need for any additional rights or contract review.

## Evidence and assumptions

There are no supplied interviews, group-listening pilots, rights schedules, concurrency terms, territorial catalog comparisons, or household eligibility rules. Existing streaming capability is established company context; authorization for this proposed package is not.

The forecast assumes households keep listening longer because of social activity. It includes no evidence that longer listening reduces churn, and no estimate of the additional royalty cost of longer or simultaneous listening. This is an evidence gap, not proof that the feature is prohibited.

## Business case

Five hundred households paying $15 monthly would generate $7,500 monthly subscription revenue. The model assumes $9 per household in royalties and $2 in infrastructure/support, leaving $4 contribution, or $2,000 monthly before acquisition and fixed costs.

At that steady level, recovering the $90,000 launch cost would take 45 months before omitted costs. Management's proposed target is twelve-month recovery, with no additional adoption or pricing scenario supplied to bridge the difference.

## Delivery and success gates

Release after playback synchronization succeeds in 95 of 100 scripted sessions and support can remove disruptive participants. The plan calls for licensing review during development but does not require written approval before release. Measure paid household renewal after one month; no retention target or comparison cohort is specified.

## Stakeholder choices still needed

Should every listener instead need a separate paid account, or should the team negotiate a household plan first? Which territory and catalog are in scope? Choosing either alternative changes the pricing, customer experience, or launch commitment and requires an explicit decision.
