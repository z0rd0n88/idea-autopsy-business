# C04 — Everpix Family Recovery Vault

Synthetic counterfactual feature proposal for an imagined operating-era Everpix team; not a recovered company document or a present-day launch request. Only the source-linked product context is factual. All feature-specific observations, costs, forecasts, staffing, and constraints below are invented test data.

## Company context

Everpix developed software for collecting and rediscovering personal photographs, including Memories and Flashback experiences. This proposal adds a recovery workflow to that photo-library setting. The source is retrospective, not evidence available to a historical decision-maker. [Everpix product timeline](https://github.com/everpix/Everpix-Intelligence/blob/master/Timeline%20%26%20Numbers.md).

## Decision requested

Approve a three-week, $15,000 offline prototype study with eight households. Allocate $8,000 engineering, $4,000 research, and $3,000 privacy and deletion testing. The fictional team has this budget reserved and one engineer available for three weeks. The product founder owns the study. No new production storage service or lifetime guarantee is authorized.

## Customer problem and feature

The family member who organizes photographs is often the only person who knows where originals are stored. Another household member may struggle to retrieve them after a lost device or account-access problem.

Family Recovery Vault lets an owner select an album, designate a second consenting adult, and generate an export package with originals and a readable index. The owner previews access before granting it. The study uses temporary local copies of explicitly selected, non-sensitive photographs; it does not transfer entire libraries, infer family relationships, or handle inheritance claims.

## Evidence and assumptions

In the fictional supplied interviews, six of eight households reported difficulty retrieving originals across devices, and five could not complete a ten-photo recovery task within ten minutes. All eight agreed to a second moderated session using the same task definition. These small, selected observations suggest a usability problem; they do not establish a market or a viable subscription business.

Participants have not agreed to pay. Production storage costs, long-term household participation, and the willingness to appoint another adult remain unknown. Existing library organization code may be reusable, but production authorization and deletion behavior would need a separate review.

## Business case

An optional $24-per-year household add-on at 1,000 subscribers would generate $24,000 annual revenue before storage, support, taxes, and payment costs. Both price and subscriber count are hypotheses. The study's purpose is to learn whether recovery is valuable enough to investigate further, not to demonstrate company-wide sustainability.

## Delivery and success gates

Build the local export prototype in week one. In weeks two and three, have the second adult retrieve ten specified originals without organizer assistance. Require at least six of eight households to finish within five minutes, all exports to match the source-file checksums, and all temporary copies to pass a documented deletion check.

The sample is a directional usability screen. Permission confusion, missing originals, or failed deletion stops the study. Passing permits a separate pricing and production-feasibility proposal; failing means archive the prototype or seek approval for a different workflow.

## Stakeholder choices still needed

Should any future service sell recovery as an add-on or include it in the core subscription? Should households or individual photographers be the buyer? Neither choice is required to run the bounded study, but neither is silently settled by it.
