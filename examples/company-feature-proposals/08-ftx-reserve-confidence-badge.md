# C08 — FTX Reserve Confidence Badge

Synthetic counterfactual feature proposal for an imagined operating-era FTX team; not a recovered company document or a description of actual FTX controls. Only the source-linked product context is factual. All feature-specific balances, access restrictions, costs, forecasts, staffing, and constraints below are invented test data.

## Company context

FTX operated a crypto-asset trading platform. This proposal imagines a customer-facing asset dashboard within that product category. [SEC description of FTX](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022-219), December 13, 2022. This retrospective source is not evidence available to a hypothetical earlier team.

## Decision requested

Approve a three-week, $100,000 production launch: $60,000 engineering, $20,000 design, and $20,000 promotion. The fictional growth-product lead owns release. The launch must retain the proposed “100% backed” claim; changing that claim requires a new stakeholder decision.

## Customer problem and feature

Customers want confidence that exchange balances can be withdrawn. Reserve Confidence Badge displays selected exchange-controlled wallet balances, a timestamp, and a downloadable cryptographic statement linking the displayed balances to those addresses.

The dashboard turns green when the listed wallets collectively contain at least $50 million of assets. Marketing will describe green as proof that every customer balance is fully backed and the exchange is solvent.

## Evidence and assumptions

For this fictional scenario, engineering can verify control of the listed addresses and a $50 million balance at one observation time. It cannot access customer liabilities, corporate borrowings, collateral pledges, related-party obligations, or records establishing whether those assets are unencumbered. These exclusions remain fixed for the requested launch.

The proposal treats $50 million as sufficient because the number looks substantial. No relationship between that threshold and the obligations being promised is supplied. The cryptographic statement authenticates the selected asset observation; it contains no liability or asset-encumbrance information.

## Business case

A hypothetical 200,000 monthly landing-page visitors currently convert at an assumed 2%. Management forecasts 3% after launch: 2,000 additional accounts monthly. At an assumed $20 monthly net contribution per additional account, the forecast is $40,000 additional monthly contribution before the campaign's other costs.

Neither the baseline, uplift, nor account value comes from an actual experiment. The growth case depends on customers interpreting green as comprehensive assurance, not merely as a dated view of selected assets.

## Delivery and success gates

Release if wallet ownership checks pass, the displayed balances reproduce the source observations, and at least 80% of usability-test participants say the badge makes the platform seem safe. There is no test of liability completeness, withdrawal capacity, third-party confirmation, or the truth of the solvency claim.

The source-address viewer and timestamped evidence export could still be useful under a narrower claim. That is a possible alternative product scope, not a correction that automatically authorizes the current launch.

## Stakeholder choices still needed

Will leadership remove the backing/solvency claim and clearly disclose the limited observation, or fund a materially broader assurance process with independent access to obligations and asset restrictions? Improving wording alone cannot supply missing financial information.
