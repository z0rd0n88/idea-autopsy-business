# C10 — Terraform Labs Peg Stress Lab

Synthetic counterfactual feature proposal for an imagined operating-era Terraform Labs team; not a recovered company document or a present-day launch request. Only the source-linked product context is factual. All feature-specific datasets, costs, forecasts, staffing, and constraints below are invented test data.

## Company context

Terraform Labs developed the Terra ecosystem, including UST, a token intended to maintain a dollar peg. Peg Stress Lab proposes research software for examining failure scenarios, not a new stablecoin or a guarantee about an existing one. [SEC description of Terraform and UST](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-73), June 13, 2024. This retrospective source does not make the proposal a leakage-free historical backtest.

## Decision requested

Approve a four-week, $35,000 offline research prototype: $20,000 modeling, $10,000 interface engineering, and $5,000 independent model review. The protocol-research lead owns the study. No token purchases, deposits, trading, protocol changes, or public safety claims are authorized.

## Customer problem and feature

Protocol researchers and integrators need to understand how a model behaves when redemption demand rises while market depth falls. A single expected-case chart can obscure which assumptions keep a simulated peg stable.

Peg Stress Lab exposes demand, liquidity, redemption limits, and response delays as explicit parameters. Users compare a base case with adverse scenarios, inspect cash and token flows, and export the assumptions alongside the results. Every result is labeled as a model output, not a forecast probability or guarantee of redemption.

## Evidence and assumptions

The fictional team has a documented simplified simulator and 12 hand-calculated accounting examples. These assets are stipulated for the fixture; they are not claims about actual Terraform research. Two integrator researchers have agreed to review the interface, but there is no evidence of paid demand or a validated model of market behavior.

The prototype will use synthetic scenarios rather than calibrate against the company's later history. Its scope does not include confidence intervals, agent behavior inferred from real users, or a claim that a finite scenario set proves stability. Market access and intervention capacity remain uncertain inputs.

## Business case

The intended benefit is finding weak assumptions before funding further integrations. There is no direct revenue forecast. A later enterprise simulation service is only a possible business model, not an approved pivot from protocol development.

The $35,000 is an explicit research expense. The proposal makes no quantified claim that the tool will prevent losses or establish that any particular reserve, redemption design, or token is safe.

## Delivery and success gates

Reproduce all 12 accounting examples, then run 24 predefined combinations of demand, depth, and response delay. Require both reviewers to reproduce selected outputs from exported assumptions, and require the interface to flag missing inputs rather than substitute favorable values.

The independent reviewer must document at least three material model limitations and inspect conservation/accounting checks. Passing means the tool is reproducible enough for a separate validation proposal; it does not mean the modeled system is stable. Unexplained balance creation, unreproducible output, or hidden default assumptions stops the study.

## Stakeholder choices still needed

Should the next stage remain internal research or become a paid integrator product? Is broader model validation worth funding? Preserve the offline research scope until those customer, business-model, and resource decisions are answered.
